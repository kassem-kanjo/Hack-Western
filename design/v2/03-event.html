design/v2/03-event.html
  1. Opens in the same screen. Click a list row or a marker: the rail swaps to the detail, the map zooms to the event. No new page, so the context stays and "Back" is one click. Breadcrumb under the bar, not a tab.
  2. Same four blocks, always the same order, so after one event the second is already readable: kind and ship, one big number, one sentence, the proof, the numbers.
  3. One big number is the evidence (24× too fast / 4 h 12 min silent). It is the most important fact about this event, at 56 px so it reads at 1 m.
  4. The physics proof is one small picture, not a chart. Jump: two bars on one scale, needed 380 kn vs maximum 16 kn. The tiny second bar is the argument.
  5. The map repeats the proof: the violet circle is how far the ship could get in 6 min; the second position is far outside it.
  6. Progressive disclosure. Numbers are visible, sources and caveats start collapsed with a count ("3"). Primary action: Replay this event (V2 explainer); in V1 the button is absent.
  7. Map labels carry times and one fact each, never a table. Positions marked with filled (known) vs hollow (the other end).
  8. Silence leads with the sentence, then proves we were listening. Two tracks on one time axis: this ship goes quiet while the other row (all other ships heard) never does. That is the whole claim.
  9. Violet zone on the map = where it could have been (up to 89 km), the same lens meaning as the Global case file. One visual language across theatres.
  10. Disclosures shown open here so the content is reviewable. Caveats are plain statements, not legalese: this is where honesty lives, one click away, never hidden.
  11. Red appears once: the sanctions-watchlist tag, next to the ship's name where an analyst looks first.
