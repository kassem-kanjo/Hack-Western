# Demo video script v2 (captions = these lines, read aloud by Kassem's friends)

Rules: no Hormuz. Every zoom is motivated: wide shot -> cursor moves -> click (ring) -> the UI changes -> zoom in on what changed while the caption explains -> hold -> zoom back out before the next action. Never zoom while the cursor is moving. One zoom per caption at most. Ease 700 ms, scale 1.3-1.8.

## 0 · Title card (keep the current opening; Kassem likes it)
(no caption on the card itself)

## 1 · The problem (wide Watch map, no zoom, slow drift is fine)
1. Every big ship broadcasts where it is, with a radio called a transponder.
2. Some switch it off, to hide where they go and who they meet.

## 2 · One ship goes dark (cursor clicks GIVING in the left list -> map flies to it -> zoom on the end of its track)
3. Last night in the Baltic, this ship, GIVING, switched off its transponder.

## 3 · Where it could be (zoom back out so the whole circle is in frame)
4. Since then, it could be anywhere inside this circle.

## 4 · Who it could have met (cursor moves to the red STILGAR label -> click -> STILGAR's panel opens -> zoom on "On a sanctions list: Yes · matched by IMO")
5. And inside that circle is STILGAR, a ship on a sanctions list.
6. It has been stopped at sea for hours.
7. Two ships, side by side, out of sight: that's how sanctioned oil moves from one hull to another.
8. We can't prove they met. We can show they could have, and where to look.
(zoom back out to the circle with both ships visible on line 8)

## 5 · Where to look (cursor clicks BALTIC STAR in the list -> panel scrolls to Satellite check -> zoom on the radar chips + red dots)
9. Satellite radar sees every ship, broadcasting or not.
10. One pass over the southern Baltic on Saturday found 7 ships that weren't broadcasting.

## 6 · Eyes on the water (cursor clicks the Patrol unit tab -> clicks "Show a recorded sighting" -> zoom on the red verdict and "Alarm in ... ms")
11. On the water, our lookout runs on QNX, on a Raspberry Pi with a camera.
12. It sees a vessel. Nothing nearby is broadcasting. It raises the alarm in under 200 milliseconds.
13. Under full load, 63 of 64 alarms still arrived on time.
(zoom out)

## End card (keep)
